# Clause library

Each clause at three positions on the strength dial. Firm favours the issuer,
Market is what a competent counterparty signs without argument, Exposed concedes
the point. Language is drafting-ready; substitute the defined terms in use.

Read the position labels from the issuer's side. If you are reviewing an
incoming document, Firm and Exposed swap.

## 1. Payment terms

| Position | Language |
|---|---|
| Firm | 50% on signature, 50% on delivery. Invoices payable within 15 days of receipt. Work may be suspended on any amount overdue by more than 7 days. |
| Market | Milestone-linked invoicing, payable within 30 days of invoice date. |
| Exposed | Payable within 60 days of month-end following acceptance, no suspension right. |

The trigger matters more than the number. "30 days from acceptance" where
acceptance has no deadline is worse than "60 days from invoice". Check trigger
and clock together, always.

## 2. Late payment interest

| Position | Language |
|---|---|
| Firm | Interest at 1.5% per month on overdue amounts, plus recovery costs, plus a right to suspend. |
| Market | Interest at 1% per month, or the statutory rate if higher. |
| Exposed | Silent. Which means chasing is a commercial conversation, not a contractual right. |

## 3. Retention / holdback

| Position | Language |
|---|---|
| Firm (as supplier) | No retention. |
| Market | 5–10% retained, released 50% on completion and 50% at the end of the defect liability period, with a fixed outer date. |
| Exposed | 10% retained with release conditional on a certificate the client issues at its discretion. |

Retention without an outer long-stop date is a permanent discount. Insert one.

## 4. Price escalation

| Position | Language |
|---|---|
| Firm | Rates revised annually by the greater of 8% or the change in the published index. |
| Market | Rates fixed for 12 months, thereafter revised by mutual agreement, failing which by the published index. |
| Exposed | Rates fixed for the full term. |

For input-cost-heavy work, add a material-price variation clause tied to a named
index with a stated base date, rather than a flat percentage.

## 5. Taxes and withholding

| Position | Language |
|---|---|
| Firm | All amounts are exclusive of taxes, which the Client bears. Any withholding is grossed up so the Supplier receives the full invoiced amount. |
| Market | Amounts exclusive of indirect tax; withholding applied at statutory rates with a certificate furnished within 30 days. |
| Exposed | Amounts inclusive of all taxes and levies, present and future. |

"Inclusive of all taxes" transfers every future rate change to the supplier.
Never accept it on a multi-year contract without a change-in-law clause.

## 6. Acceptance and deemed acceptance

| Position | Language |
|---|---|
| Firm | Deliverables are deemed accepted 5 business days after submission unless rejected in writing with specific reference to the acceptance criteria. |
| Market | 10 business days, one round of rectification, then deemed accepted. |
| Exposed | Acceptance at the Client's satisfaction, no deadline. |

"At the Client's satisfaction" with no objective criteria and no clock is the
single most common cause of unpaid completed work.

## 7. Change orders

| Position | Language |
|---|---|
| Firm | Any change to scope requires a signed change order stating price and time impact. Work on unsigned changes is not performed. |
| Market | Changes requested in writing; the Supplier responds within 5 business days with impact; no work proceeds until signed. |
| Exposed | Minor changes absorbed within the existing fee at the Supplier's cost. |

Define "minor" numerically or delete the word. Undefined, it expands to fit the
client's budget.

## 8. Client dependencies

| Position | Language |
|---|---|
| Firm | Listed dependencies. Failure to meet one extends all dates day-for-day and entitles the Supplier to recover standby costs at **[rate]**. |
| Market | Listed dependencies; failure extends dates day-for-day. |
| Exposed | Silent. |

Day-for-day extension without a cost recovery still leaves you carrying an idle
team. Price standby explicitly on resource-heavy engagements.

## 9. Exclusions

| Position | Language |
|---|---|
| Firm | An itemised exclusions list, plus: anything not expressly stated as in scope is out of scope. |
| Market | An itemised exclusions list. |
| Exposed | Silent, with scope described in general narrative terms. |

The catch-all sentence in Firm costs nothing and closes the largest hole in
every services document.

## 10. Term and renewal

| Position | Language |
|---|---|
| Firm | Initial term of **[period]**, auto-renewing for successive equal terms unless either party gives 60 days' notice, with rates revised on renewal. |
| Market | Fixed term, renewal by mutual written agreement. |
| Exposed | Auto-renewal with 180 days' notice required to exit and rates held flat. |

Auto-renewal is fine. Auto-renewal plus a long notice window plus a lock-in is
how a one-year commitment becomes a three-year one.

## 11. Termination for convenience

| Position | Language |
|---|---|
| Firm (as supplier) | Client may terminate on 90 days' notice, paying for work performed, committed third-party costs, and demobilisation. |
| Market | Either party on 30–60 days' notice; work performed to date is payable. |
| Exposed | Client may terminate immediately with no compensation beyond delivered work. |

Asymmetry check: if they can leave on 7 days and you owe 90, that is a finding.
Either match the periods or price the exposure.

## 12. Termination for cause and cure periods

| Position | Language |
|---|---|
| Firm | Termination on material breach not cured within 30 days of written notice; immediate termination for insolvency or non-payment beyond 15 days. |
| Market | 30-day cure period for material breach, both directions. |
| Exposed | Immediate termination at the Client's determination of breach, no cure period. |

A cure period that allows the counterparty to determine breach unilaterally is a
termination-for-convenience clause wearing a costume.

## 13. Consequences of termination

Covers: payment for work in progress, return of materials, transition assistance
and its rate, licence survival for deliverables already paid for, and the
survival list. Termination clauses without a survival list leave confidentiality
and IP assignment arguably dead on termination, which is the opposite of the
intent.

## 14. Limitation of liability

| Position | Language |
|---|---|
| Firm | Aggregate liability capped at fees paid in the 3 months preceding the claim. No liability for indirect, consequential, special or punitive loss, or loss of profit, revenue, data or goodwill. |
| Market | Capped at fees paid in the preceding 12 months, or the contract value, whichever is lower. Same indirect-loss exclusion. |
| Exposed | Uncapped, or capped at a multiple of contract value. |

Test the cap against realistic downside, not against the fee. A ₹5 lakh
engagement that can cause a ₹2 crore outage needs the conversation had at
signature, not at the incident.

## 15. Liability carve-outs

Standard carve-outs from the cap: fraud, wilful misconduct, death or personal
injury, breach of confidentiality, IP infringement indemnity, and unpaid fees.
Firm keeps carve-outs to fraud and personal injury. Market accepts the full
list. Exposed adds data breach uncapped, which in a data-heavy engagement means
the cap does not exist.

## 16. Indemnity

| Position | Language |
|---|---|
| Firm | Supplier indemnifies only for third-party IP infringement claims arising from the deliverables, capped at the liability cap, conditional on prompt notice and conduct of defence. |
| Market | Adds indemnity for breach of confidentiality and for personal injury caused by the Supplier's personnel. |
| Exposed | Broad indemnity for "any claim arising out of or in connection with" the services, uncapped. |

An uncapped indemnity defeats a capped liability clause. Where both appear,
check whether the indemnity is expressly subject to the cap. Usually it is not,
and usually that is deliberate.

## 17. Warranties

| Position | Language |
|---|---|
| Firm | Services performed with reasonable skill and care. All other warranties, express or implied, disclaimed to the extent permitted by law. |
| Market | Adds: deliverables will materially conform to the agreed specification for **[90]** days, remedy limited to re-performance. |
| Exposed | Fitness for the Client's particular purpose, merchantability, and error-free operation. |

"Error-free" is not achievable in software and should never be warranted.

## 18. IP ownership

| Position | Language |
|---|---|
| Firm | Supplier retains all IP; Client receives a non-exclusive, perpetual licence to use deliverables for internal purposes. |
| Market | Foreground IP in the deliverables assigns to the Client on full payment; Supplier retains background IP and grants a licence to use it within the deliverables. |
| Exposed | Full assignment of all IP including background, tools and methodology, effective on creation. |

Two details decide this clause. Assignment "on full payment" rather than "on
creation" keeps leverage. And an express carve-out for generic know-how,
methodologies and reusable components protects the ability to do the next
project at all.

## 19. Background IP and licences

State expressly what is background, ideally in a schedule. Without the schedule,
the boundary between background and foreground is decided later, by whoever has
more appetite for the argument.

## 20. Confidentiality survival

| Position | Language |
|---|---|
| Firm | Obligations survive 5 years from disclosure; trade secrets survive indefinitely. |
| Market | 3 years from termination; trade secrets indefinite. |
| Exposed | Perpetual on all information without exception. |

Perpetual-on-everything is unadministrable, which makes it unenforceable in
practice and dangerous in principle: it cannot be complied with, so breach is
permanent.

## 21. Data protection and security

Covers: role of each party, permitted processing purposes, security standards by
reference to a named framework, sub-processor consent, breach notification
window, audit rights, data location, deletion on termination. Firm caps audit
rights at once a year on notice at the auditing party's cost. Exposed grants
unlimited audit at any time, which for a small supplier is a real operating cost.

## 22. Non-solicitation

| Position | Language |
|---|---|
| Firm | Neither party solicits or employs the other's personnel involved in the engagement during the term and for 12 months after, with liquidated damages of **[amount or multiple of salary]**. |
| Market | Same restriction, 12 months, no liquidated damages, with a carve-out for general public advertisements. |
| Exposed | One-way restriction binding only the Supplier. |

Always carve out responses to general advertisements. Without it, the clause
catches hires nobody solicited and becomes a trap rather than a protection.

## 23. Post-termination restraint

Jurisdiction-dependent and frequently void. In India, an agreement restraining a
person from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business is void under
section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, with a narrow exception around the sale
of goodwill. During employment, restraints are generally sustainable; after it,
generally not. Draft confidentiality and non-solicitation instead, which do
survive, and do not copy a US-style non-compete into an Indian document and
assume it operates.

## 24. Force majeure

| Position | Language |
|---|---|
| Firm | Defined event list plus a catch-all, relief from performance, payment obligations unaffected, termination right for either party if the event continues beyond 60 days. |
| Market | Same, with payment obligations also suspended for affected services. |
| Exposed | No termination right, indefinite suspension. |

Two things force majeure clauses routinely omit: whether payment obligations are
suspended, and a long-stop that lets either side exit. Both matter more than the
event list.

## 25. Assignment and subcontracting

Firm: the Supplier may subcontract on notice; neither party may assign without
consent, not to be unreasonably withheld, with a carve-out for group companies
and for a sale of the business. Exposed: the Client may assign freely, including
to a competitor, while the Supplier may not subcontract at all.

## 26. Dispute resolution

| Position | Language |
|---|---|
| Firm | Escalation to named senior executives for 15 days, then arbitration by a sole arbitrator, seat at **[city]**, in English, award final and binding. |
| Market | Same, with the arbitrator appointed by agreement, failing which under the applicable institutional rules. |
| Exposed | Courts at the counterparty's location, with no escalation step. |

State the **seat**, not only the venue: the seat determines the supervisory
court. A clause that names a venue but no seat generates a preliminary dispute
about the dispute clause.

## 27. Entire agreement and variation

Entire agreement clause, plus: variation only in writing signed by both parties,
plus a no-waiver clause. Without the variation clause, a course of conduct can
amend a contract that both sides believe is fixed — the most common route by
which a written payment term quietly becomes the one everybody actually follows.
