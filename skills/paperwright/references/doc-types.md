## 1. Choosing the instrument

| The situation | The instrument | Not this |
|---|---|---|
| Ongoing relationship, many future projects | MSA + SOW per project | One giant contract |
| One project, fixed scope, no repeat expected | Standalone services agreement | MSA (overhead for nothing) |
| Talking before a deal exists | NDA, then MOU | Draft contract (implies terms are settled) |
| Individual doing work, not on payroll | Contractor agreement | Offer letter (creates employment risk) |
| Scope grew mid-project | Change order under the SOW | New SOW (orphans the original terms) |
| Supplier missed the spec | Rectification notice under the contract | Angry email (waives the contractual machinery) |
| Deal is dead and money is disputed | Settlement with full-and-final discharge | Termination notice alone (leaves claims live) |

Two failure patterns dominate. First, using an NDA where a services agreement
was needed: confidentiality is covered and the work itself is governed by
nothing. Second, a series of quotes and emails treated as the contract, which
means the terms are whatever the last email said, in whatever order a court
reads them.

## 2. Statement of Work (SOW)

Governs one project. Sits under an MSA, or carries its own terms if standalone.

**Mandatory clause set**

1. Parties and the governing agreement it sits under (or "this SOW is the entire agreement").
2. Background and objective — one paragraph, no marketing language.
3. Scope of services, itemised, each line a deliverable a person could tick off.
4. Express exclusions — the single most valuable clause in a SOW.
5. Deliverables schedule with dates or elapsed-time triggers.
6. Acceptance criteria and the acceptance procedure, including deemed acceptance.
7. Client dependencies and the consequence of delay in meeting them.
8. Fees, the payment trigger, and the invoicing schedule.
9. Taxes, expenses, and who bears withholding.
10. Change-order procedure with a price and time impact mechanism.
11. Key personnel and substitution rights, where the client is buying people.
12. Term, termination, and what is payable on early termination.
13. IP ownership split: background, foreground, and third-party components.
14. Signature blocks with name, title and date.

**Usually left out, usually expensive**

1. *Deemed acceptance.* Without it, a client who never signs off never triggers payment.
2. *Dependency failure consequence.* "Client will provide access" with no consequence means the delay is yours.
3. *Exclusions.* Everything not excluded is arguably included, and arguable is where margin dies.
4. *Assumption register.* State the assumptions the price rests on: headcount available, one round of revisions, environments provided. If an assumption breaks, that is a change order, not goodwill.

**Wrong instrument signal.** If the scope is "we will help with whatever comes
up", a SOW is the wrong shape — write a retainer with a capped hours schedule
and an overage rate.

## 3. Master Services Agreement (MSA)

The stable layer. Legal terms that do not change project to project, so each SOW
can be two pages instead of twenty.

**Mandatory clause set**

1. Definitions.
2. Structure clause: how SOWs are raised, and the order of precedence between MSA and SOW.
3. Term and renewal.
4. Fees, invoicing, late payment interest, set-off.
5. Taxes and withholding.
6. Confidentiality.
7. IP ownership framework.
8. Data protection and security obligations.
9. Warranties — and their limits.
10. Limitation of liability with cap and carve-outs.
11. Indemnities.
12. Insurance, where the client requires it.
13. Termination for convenience and for cause, with cure periods.
14. Consequences of termination and survival.
15. Non-solicitation of personnel.
16. Force majeure.
17. Assignment and subcontracting.
18. Notices.
19. Governing law and dispute resolution.
20. Entire agreement, variation, severability, counterparts.

**Order of precedence is the clause that gets forgotten.** State whether the SOW
or the MSA wins on conflict. Most sensible answer: the MSA wins on legal terms,
the SOW wins on commercial and scope terms, and the SOW may vary MSA terms only
where it says so expressly and both parties sign.

## 4. Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA)

**Mandatory clause set**

1. Parties and direction (one-way or mutual — mutual by default unless only one side is disclosing).
2. Definition of Confidential Information, and the standard exclusions: already public, already known, independently developed, lawfully received from a third party.
3. Permitted purpose, narrowly stated. This is the clause that does the work.
4. Permitted recipients, and responsibility for their breach.
5. Compelled-disclosure carve-out with a notice obligation.
6. Term of the agreement and, separately, the survival period of the obligation.
7. Return or destruction on request, with a carve-out for backups and regulatory retention.
8. No licence, no warranty as to accuracy, no obligation to proceed.
9. Remedies: injunctive relief.
10. Governing law and jurisdiction.

**The two mistakes.** Confusing the term of the NDA with the survival period of
confidentiality — a two-year NDA can carry a five-year survival. And an
unlimited perpetual obligation on everything, which is unenforceable in practice
and unadministrable in reality; carve trade secrets out for perpetual protection
and time-limit the rest.

## 5. Independent Contractor / Consultancy Agreement

**Mandatory clause set**

1. Parties, and an express statement that the relationship is not employment.
2. Services and deliverables.
3. Fees, rate basis, invoicing, expenses.
4. Term and termination.
5. Contractor's own tax, statutory contribution and registration responsibility.
6. IP assignment — present-tense assignment of all work product, plus a further-assurance clause and a waiver of moral rights where the jurisdiction allows.
7. Confidentiality.
8. No authority to bind.
9. Own tools, own hours, right of substitution (or the absence of it, deliberately).
10. Non-solicitation, scoped by time and by named clients.
11. Indemnity for misclassification claims.
12. Governing law.

**The classification risk is the whole point.** Fixed hours, exclusive
engagement, company equipment, a reporting line and integration into the team
make the label irrelevant. Draft the substance to match the label, or accept
that the label will not hold.

## 6. Offer Letter and Employment Terms

**Mandatory clause set**

1. Position, reporting line, location, and whether remote or hybrid is a term or a discretion.
2. Start date and the conditions the offer is subject to: background check, references, document verification.
3. Compensation broken into fixed, variable and benefits, with the variable's measurement basis and payment timing.
4. Equity, if any: instrument, quantity or percentage basis, vesting schedule, cliff, exercise window, treatment on termination.
5. Probation period and the notice applicable during it.
6. Notice period post-confirmation, both directions, and any buyout.
7. Working hours and leave.
8. Confidentiality and IP assignment.
9. Post-termination restraints — drafted to the jurisdiction's limits, not copied from a US template.
10. At-will or notice-based framing, matched to the jurisdiction.
11. Governing law and acceptance mechanism with a deadline.

**Left out, later litigated.** Treatment of unvested equity on termination for
cause versus without cause versus resignation. The variable pay measurement
basis, which becomes a dispute at the first payout. And whether the offer
supersedes everything said during the process — it should say so expressly.

## 7. Work Order / Purchase Order terms

Short-form commitment, usually against a rate card or a prior quotation.

1. Reference to the quotation or rate card, by number and date.
2. Scope in one table: item, quantity, unit rate, amount.
3. Delivery or completion date and site.
4. Payment terms and retention, if any.
5. Defect liability period and rectification obligation.
6. Price basis: firm, or variable with a named index.
7. Taxes, and whether rates are inclusive or exclusive.
8. Incorporation of standard terms by reference, with those terms attached.

**The trap.** A purchase order that merely says "as per your quotation dated X"
imports the vendor's terms, including their liability cap and their payment
terms. Either attach your own terms and state they prevail, or accept theirs
knowingly.

## 8. Lease Heads of Terms and Leave & Licence

Heads of terms are non-binding except for the clauses stated to be binding.

1. Premises, with area basis stated: carpet, built-up or super built-up. Areas are the most common source of disagreement.
2. Term, lock-in for each party, and renewal option with the escalation basis.
3. Rent, escalation percentage and frequency, rent-free fit-out period.
4. Security deposit, interest, and the refund timeline with a deduction mechanism.
5. Permitted use, drafted to the tenant's actual operation.
6. Fit-out rights, landlord approval, reinstatement obligation at exit.
7. Maintenance, common area charges, and who bears major structural repair.
8. Utilities, load sanctioned, and who pays for augmentation.
9. Taxes, property tax responsibility, GST on rent.
10. Assignment and sub-licensing.
11. Termination, notice, and consequences during lock-in.
12. Binding clauses: exclusivity period, confidentiality, costs.

**Instrument choice matters here.** A leave and licence arrangement and a lease
have different registration, stamping and possession consequences. Name the one
intended, and draft consistently with it — a document titled licence that grants
exclusive possession for nine years is a lease whatever the title says.

## 9. Memorandum of Understanding (MOU)

For recording alignment before a contract exists.

1. Parties and context.
2. Shared objective.
3. Intended contributions of each side, framed as intentions, not obligations.
4. Exclusivity, if any, with a hard end date.
5. Confidentiality — binding.
6. Costs — each side bears its own.
7. An express non-binding clause naming exactly which clauses *are* binding.
8. Term and lapse date.

**Write the non-binding clause first.** An MOU without it is a contract with
vague obligations, which is the worst of both.

## 10. Change Order / Amendment

1. Reference to the original agreement by title, date and parties.
2. The change, stated as a redline instruction: clause X is deleted and replaced with the following.
3. Price impact, stated as a delta and a revised total.
4. Time impact, stated as a delta and a revised date.
5. Confirmation that all other terms remain unchanged.
6. Effective date.
7. Signatures from the same signing authority as the original.

**Never rewrite the whole agreement to make one change** unless the amendments
have accumulated past readability — then issue an amended and restated version,
and say clearly that it supersedes.

## 11. Defect, Breach and Rectification Notice

The document that preserves your rights. It is a legal step, not a complaint.

1. Addressee, with the notice clause's address and method followed exactly.
2. Reference to the contract, by title and date.
3. The specific clause breached, quoted.
4. The facts: what was supposed to happen, what happened, with dates.
5. The defect schedule, itemised, each with location, description and the standard it fails.
6. Evidence reference: photographs, inspection reports, prior correspondence, each dated.
7. The cure period, taken from the contract, not invented.
8. What is required to cure, stated so that compliance is objectively testable.
9. The consequence if not cured, drawn from the contract's own remedies.
10. Reservation of rights.
11. Delivery method and date.

**Follow the contract's notice clause exactly.** Email where the contract
requires registered post can invalidate the notice and restart the clock. Tone
is neutral throughout — a notice that reads as angry reads as weak.

---

## 12. Termination Notice

1. Contract reference.
2. The termination clause relied on, and which limb: convenience or cause.
3. If cause: the breach, the prior notice, and the expiry of the cure period.
4. Effective date of termination, calculated against the notice period.
5. Transition obligations: handover, deliverables in progress, access revocation.
6. Reconciliation of amounts: what has been invoiced, what is payable, what is disputed.
7. Surviving clauses, listed.
8. Reservation of rights, and an express statement that termination is not a waiver.

---

## 13. Settlement and Full-and-Final Discharge

1. Recitals: the dispute, the contract, the claims.
2. Settlement amount, payment mechanism and date.
3. Mutual release, scoped by date and subject matter.
4. Carve-outs: fraud, and claims that cannot be released by law.
5. No admission of liability.
6. Confidentiality of the settlement.
7. Non-disparagement, if wanted, drafted mutually.
8. Withdrawal of any proceedings, with who files what.
9. Entire agreement and supersession of the underlying contract.
10. Governing law and dispute resolution for the settlement itself.

**Sequence the release against the payment.** A release effective on signature
with payment due in 30 days leaves the paying party released and unpaid-for.
Make the release conditional on receipt of cleared funds.
